Over two months, I travelled to Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Vietnam with about 26 litres worth of livelihood on my back. This was an experience
I would recommend everyone do at least once. I've compiled a set of thoughts about the trip here that are by no means exhaustive. If you want to know more
or otherwise need help packing with limited space, feel free to reach out! I can talk about this trip for days.

I spent five days in Singapore total, mostly as an homage to my younger self. I spent the first 14 years of my life in this country and had some
friends here that I haven't seen in forever. It was about what I remember: a hot and humid place with some great food.
I unfortunately couldn't afford to stay here for much longer than these five days.

Probably my favourite place on earth. I was in Thailand for just north of three weeks. Namely, I went to Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, and Koh Tao. I
spent most of this time on Koh Tao. Fantastic food, training Muay Thai, and lots of snorkelling made these three weeks a paradise.

If you were thinking of heading to Thailand, Koh Tao is unskippable. Try the Massaman Curry at Lotus, or 995 Duck, and have as many passionfruit smoothies
as you are physically capable of.

I spent exactly three weeks in Indonesia, with about 4 days in Gili Trawangan, 2 weeks being in Lombok and the rest in Bali.
Bali was overrated in my view, but Indonesia as a whole was awesome. Got my Open Water in Gili T, went up Mt Rinjani and surfed loads in Lombok,
and listened to some live music in Bali.

I was in Vietnam for two weeks. I went to Hanoi, did the Ha Giang loop, went to Cat Ba island, Da Nang and Hoi An. I'm not the first to tell you about the
coffee, but I might be the first to recommend Cat Ba over Ha Long Bay. Also, visit Duong's Restaurant in Hanoi. Michelin star, amazing food for cheap.
What's not to love?

---

There's plenty of people online who will rave about their solo travelling being a life changing endeavour; that travelling made them more independent, more confident,
more capable. While I can attest minorly to those statements, the outcome of being a 'better' person (however you define that) isn't the goal of the trip.
The goal is the adventure - to wander around, goalless and in wanderlust, and meet people from all paths of life doing the same thing. The point of doing this
is to experience a new perspective, to temporarily exit the known and exist in a completely unpredictable environment.

I highly recommend that you consider a similar trip.
